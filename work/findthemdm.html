Case study · Web platform

Find Them DM

Dominica's missing-person alerts lived on social media, where they scrolled away within hours and outdated posts kept circulating after people were found. Find Them DM gives every case one permanent, searchable, shareable home, and gives the people managing them a secure place to work.

Find Them DM · demo

The problem

When someone goes missing in Dominica, the alert spreads through Facebook and WhatsApp. That reach is valuable, but posts are hard to find a day later, details change between copies, and nobody can tell whether a forwarded alert is still current.

The goal was a single trusted place: easy to search on any phone, built to be shared, clear about who has been found, and run by a small team who must be confident nothing goes public by mistake.

The public site

Search & filters

Search by name, nickname or community. Filter by alert type, parish and dates.

Colour-coded alerts

Cases follow the national alert classifications, so each one shows at a glance who is missing.

Share anywhere

One-tap WhatsApp, Facebook and X sharing, plus printable posters with a QR code to the live page.

"Located" banners

Found people are clearly marked, so old alerts stop circulating, with a public timeline of updates.

Feeds for media

RSS feeds let newsrooms and partners pick up new alerts and updates automatically.

Fast on any connection

Server-rendered pages with no heavy front-end framework, so they load quickly on mobile data.

The staff portal

Behind the public site is a portal where staff create and manage cases. Nothing reaches the public without a second pair of eyes: cases move from draft to review to approval to publication, and the person who submits a case can't approve it.

Staff have roles (case managers, publishers and auditors). Sign-in uses two-factor authentication, and an audit trail records every change, by whom and why. Edits to live cases wait for approval, uploaded photos have their location data stripped, and private notes never appear publicly.

Everything an agency would want to change (name, logo, colours, police contact) is a setting, so the platform can be adopted by an official body without rewriting code. A separate demo mode with fictional records lets prospective partners try the whole workflow safely.
